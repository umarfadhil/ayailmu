# Bahasa / Languages — Syllabus

**Status:** DRAFT v2 2026-10-16 (enriched: essentials gaps closed, hidden gems added). Framework, levels, activity types, bilingual model and the gem mechanic are in `00_FRAMEWORK.md`. Build order: **Arab (now) → Inggris → Bahasa Indonesia (Literasi) → Indonesian for English speakers → Jepang/Korea/Mandarin (planned)**. Gem facts carry a `source` note in the pack and are cut if a reviewer cannot verify them.

Cross-track essentials (every language track, every level): a counted word list; listening on every unit (`audio_match`, bundled clips); pronunciation traps specific to Indonesian speakers (or to English speakers for track D); a reading step; a writing step from Menengah; one "Kenapa penting" unit; one making prompt (record yourself, write a note, label your room).

Hidden gems (kids): "Indonesia" comes from Greek *Indos* + *nesos*, "Indian islands" · Indonesia has 700+ living languages, more than almost any country · *bakso* and *mi* came with Chinese traders, *sepatu* and *meja* from Portuguese, *kantor* from Dutch, *guru* from Sanskrit · in 1928 young people chose Malay, not the biggest language Javanese, so nobody's language would rule the others · reduplication makes plurals: *buku-buku* · "ABCD" in Indonesian is pronounced like Dutch, that's why "C" is "cé" · the word *merdeka* traces back to Sanskrit *maharddhika*, "very prosperous".

Hidden gems (adults): Malay was a trading lingua franca for centuries, which is why its grammar is "light" · the 1972 spelling reform turned *tj* into *c* and *dj* into *j* overnight (Soekarno → Sukarno) · Indonesian has no grammatical tense or gender, yet precise time words · *jam karet* is not in KBBI as an idiom entry but everyone understands it · *Anda* was coined in the 1950s to fill a politeness gap · many "English" tech terms have official Indonesian equivalents (*unduh*, *unggah*, *tetikus*), some adopted, some not · *gotong royong* has no one-word English equivalent · Indonesian sign language (BISINDO) is a full language, not a code for Indonesian.

Hidden gems: *bisa* means both "can" and "venom" (two different origins) · there is no word for "the" and no plural -s, and nobody misses them · *kita* vs *kami*: Indonesian distinguishes "we including you" from "we not you" · *jam karet* ("rubber time") · Indonesian is one of the easiest major languages to start and one of the hardest to sound native in (affixes, register) · *mati lampu* means the power is out, not that the lamp died · the *-nya* suffix does about five jobs · Indonesians count floors from 1, and "lantai dasar" is ground.
