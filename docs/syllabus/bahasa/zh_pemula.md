## 0. How this level works

- **Target `zh`: Simplified characters, Mainland standard Putonghua; instruction `id` and `en`.** Every
  learner-facing text has both halves. Kids are addressed with "kamu", adults with "Anda".
- **Pinyin with tone marks, citation tones.** Each syllable carries its dictionary tone; tone sandhi is not written
  (你好 nǐ hǎo, 一个 yī gè, 不客气 bù kèqi, 一杯 yī bēi), neutral-tone syllables have no mark (谢谢 xièxie, 妈妈
  māma, 朋友 péngyou, 多少 duōshao). The adult unit 01 screen explains the 3+3 change once ("你好 nǐ hǎo sounds
  like ní hǎo"); the voice notes tell the speaker to say the natural spoken form. Sentences start with a capital
  (Wǒ bā suì.), names are capitalised (Wáng Míng, Shànghǎi), 十二 is written shí'èr. Never tone numbers.
- **Who sees pinyin.** Kids: on every character, everywhere (cards, model answers, help lines, screens). Adults:
  everywhere in units 01–03; in units 04–05 the decision is made **per item**: if one Chinese card in the item is new
  (first shown in 04–05), every Chinese card in it keeps its pinyin, so a lone pinyin card never marks the answer;
  an item made only of review words drops it. In this draft every unit-04/05 item contains a new word, so in practice
  adults still see pinyin throughout; text lines (model answers, screens) always give it.
- **Characters.** The 25 characters with stroke data (contract §4) are traced once per pack, when first used
  (table in §4). Every other character appears only inside whole words, always with pinyin and sound. Pemula does
  not test reading a character without pinyin (open question 1).
- **Clip keys** (contract §3.1): `zh/` + the pinyin without tone marks, one `_` between pinyin words, ü written v
  (`zh/ni_hao`, `zh/pingguo`, `zh/xiexie`, `zh/nvhai`, `zh/ni_you_xiongdi_jiemei_ma`). One-syllable words that
  would share a key get their tone number: `zh/shi2` 十 / `zh/shi4` 是, `zh/qi1` 七 / `zh/qi2` 骑. The tone drill
  uses real characters, one per tone: `zh/ma1` 妈, `zh/ma2` 麻, `zh/ma3` 马, `zh/ma4` 骂. Clip text = the Simplified
  characters exactly as spoken (sentences keep 。 or ？ for intonation); the note starts with the pinyin.
- **Picture first** (contract §1): kids 100 %, adults over 90 % of PRACT items are trace, picture, gesture or
  listening items. One shared set of prompts of at most 6 words ("Dengar, lalu ketuk gambarnya.", "Seret gambar ke
  katanya.", "Nada yang mana?", "Dibaca seperti apa?").
- **Sound.** Every Chinese card carries its clip (`audio`), except the cards in an `audio_match` (a speaker there
  would play the answer). `audio_match` items stay hidden until the ElevenLabs clips exist; **no REMED item is an
  `audio_match`**, every skill has a scored PRACT item that works without audio, every unit keeps at least 4 scored
  items without audio, and no more than two trace items follow each other when the listening items are hidden.
- **No give-aways.** Chinese cards never show their meaning (the 吃/喝 and 坐/骑 baskets show only the word, its
  pinyin and its speaker). Picture labels appear only where a picture alone is ambiguous (older/younger siblings:
  "kakak perempuan", "adik laki-laki") and never contain Chinese or pinyin. In tone items the option cards are bare
  pinyin (mā má mǎ mà).
- **Gestures:** `match_pairs` with `drag` (picture onto word) or `connect` (tone marks to contours, number words to
  digits); `sort_buckets`, `order`, `tiles` and `fill_blank` with `drag`. All work tap-then-tap too (CR-A11Y).

## 6. Mistakes Indonesian speakers make (and where the packs address them)

1. **Tones at all.** Indonesian does not use pitch to tell words apart, so learners drop tones or copy sentence
   intonation. Both screens of unit 01 show 妈 mā / 马 mǎ; tone listening (kids mà and mǎ-vs-mā with pictures; adults
   má), tone marks ↔ contours, ordering mā má mǎ mà, REMED "which tone?" and 马 → horse.
2. **Tone 2 vs tone 3.** Tone 2 rises from mid to high; tone 3 starts low (said alone it dips, then rises). Learners
   confuse má with mǎ, and say a full dip on every third tone. Adults' first listening item is má with the model
   answer "not mǎ"; 买 mǎi vs 卖 mài (unit 04).
3. **zh/ch/sh vs z/c/s.** Learners read pinyin c as Indonesian c (it is ts), and do not curl the tongue for zh ch sh.
   Minimal pairs used: 四 sì vs 十 shí (kids and adults unit 02), 山 shān vs 三 sān (kids 05), 吃 chī vs 七 qī
   (kids 02, adults 01), the adult c/q/x/zh matching.
4. **j/q/x.** q is read k or kw, x is read ks. Adult matching item (q ≈ c with a puff of air, x ≈ sy).
5. **ü.** After j, q, x, y the letter u is ü (鱼 yú, 去 qù); 女 nǚ shows the dots. Adult listening item yú vs wú vs
   yóu and REMED "how is it said?".
6. **b/d/g are not voiced.** Pinyin b, d, g are unaspirated voiceless sounds, p, t, k aspirated; Indonesian b, d, g
   are voiced. Not drilled at Pemula; reviewers should listen for it in the clips (爸爸 bàba, 大 dà, 哥哥 gēge).
7. **Pinyin e.** e in 喝 hē, 哥哥 gēge, 个 gè is not Indonesian é; closer to the pepet e of *emas*, said further
   back. Audio only at Pemula.
8. **Sandhi.** 3+3 (你好), 不 before tone 4 (不客气 bú kèqi), 一 before other tones (一个 yí ge). Pinyin stays in
   citation tones; the adult screen explains 3+3; voice notes describe the spoken form.
9. **Measure words.** Indonesian has *se-* + noun (secangkir, semangkuk), which helps: 一杯 a cup, 一碗 a bowl. Traps:
   两个 not 二个 (adult unit 02 tiles + REMED), 口 for family members (unit 03).
10. **吃 vs 喝.** Indonesian *makan sup*; Mandarin 喝汤 (adult sort item puts soup under 喝).
11. **Word order of means of transport.** Indonesian puts it last (ke Shanghai naik kereta), Mandarin before the verb
    of going (坐火车去上海): adult unit 05 tiles.
12. **他/她.** Same sound tā, like Indonesian *dia*; only the character differs (adult unit 03 screen).
