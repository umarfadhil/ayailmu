## 0. How this level works

- **Target `ko`, standard Seoul Korean; instruction `id` and `en`.** Every learner-facing text is filled in both.
- **Script first.** All 24 basic jamo are traced when first taught: the 10 vowels and the silent/ng ㅇ in unit 01,
  the plain consonants ㄱ ㄴ ㄷ ㄹ ㅁ ㅂ ㅅ ㅈ ㅎ in unit 02, the aspirated ㅋ ㅌ ㅍ ㅊ in unit 03 (taught as "the
  same letter plus a stroke, plus a puff of air", next to the tense ㄲ ㄸ ㅃ ㅆ ㅉ, a common beginner-textbook
  order). Tense consonants and compound vowels (ㅐ ㅔ ㅘ ㅝ ㅢ …) appear only inside words, always with
  romanization; they get their own tracing at Menengah (there are no stroke keys for them yet).
- **Reading aid = Revised Romanization (RR, 2000)**, lower case, following pronunciation for liaison and
  nasalisation (할아버지 harabeoji, 감사합니다 gamsahamnida, 음료 eumnyo), never showing tensing (국수 guksu).
  Kids see it on every hangul card in every unit. Adults see it on everything in units 01–03 and, from unit 04, only
  where a word is new in that unit, decided **per item**: if one card in an item is new, every card in it is
  romanized, so a lone romanized card never marks the answer (QA pass 2026-10-18). Cards in a listening item
  (`audio_match`) and letter/syllable tiles in a spelling item carry **no** romanization, since it would give the
  answer away. `check.py` recomputes every romanization from the hangul and enforces these rules, so the RR in the
  packs, the screens and the model answers is mechanically consistent.
- **Single letters:** a vowel card shows its sound (ㅓ eo) and its speaker says the syllable (어). A consonant card
  shows its sound value (ㄱ g, ㅇ ng) and its speaker says the letter's **name** (기역 giyeok, 이응 ieung), the way
  Korean children recite it; the model answer gives both ("ㄱ namanya 기역 (giyeok). Bunyinya g, seperti 가 (ga).").
- **Less text** (contract §1): one shared prompt set of at most 6 words ("Ikuti garis hurufnya.", "Dengar, lalu
  ketuk gambarnya.", "Pasangkan gambar dan katanya."); the picture, the hangul card and the sound carry the
  question. Model answers are one line; each unit has one lesson screen (unit 03 has two: blocks, family) with at
  most 3 short lines and 3–5 examples.
- **Gestures:** `match_pairs` with `drag` (picture onto word) or `connect` (number words to digits, animals to their
  sounds, places to pictures); `sort_buckets`, `order`, `tiles` and `fill_blank` with `drag`. Tiles build syllable
  blocks from letters (ㅅ + ㅗ → 소, ㄱ + ㅗ + ㅇ → 공), words from syllables (우 + 유 → 우유) and requests from words
  (빵 + 주세요). A spelling item shows the picture and the reading as a help line ("Bunyinya: so"), never the hangul
  word it asks for. All work tap-then-tap too (CR-A11Y).
- **Sound:** every Korean word or phrase on a card carries its clip (`audio`); single-letter tiles, syllable pieces
  and basket labels do not, nor do the cards of an `audio_match` (a speaker there would play the answer).
  `audio_match` items ("listen, then tap the picture / the letter") stay hidden until the
  ElevenLabs clips exist; no REMED item is an `audio_match`, so every skill keeps its follow-up without audio.
- **Picture cards** carry a bilingual `text` that is only their spoken (TalkBack) label.
- **Politeness:** polite 해요체 by default (주세요, 이에요/예요, 어디예요?); 합니다-style only for fixed phrases
  (감사합니다, 죄송합니다, 잘 먹겠습니다). Kids also get the casual 안녕 and 미안해 for friends, with the rule "to
  grown-ups: 안녕하세요".
- **Numbers:** both systems 1–10 in unit 02. Native (하나 … 열) for counting things and age; Sino-Korean (일 … 십)
  for money, dates and phone numbers (0 = 공 when reading digits). Taught as two "families" with a sort item.
  "How many?" choices offer native numbers only: 삼 next to 셋 would be a second answer that also means three.
- **Speaker-dependent sibling words** (언니/오빠 said by girls and women, 누나/형 said by boys and men): one lesson
  screen and one gentle sort item ("who uses these words?"), not tested further.

## 5. Common mistakes of Indonesian speakers at this level

1. **ㅓ (eo) vs ㅗ (o).** Indonesian has one "o"; learners say 서울 as "Soul". ㅗ = lips rounded and forward;
   ㅓ = mouth open as for "a", lips not rounded. Listening items: 어 → ㅓ/ㅗ/ㅏ (kids), 오 → ㅗ/ㅓ/ㅜ (adults).
2. **ㅡ (eu) said as ㅜ (u).** ㅡ is "u with a smile" (lips spread). Reading RR "eu" as two sounds "e-u" is a second
   trap: it is one vowel.
3. **Three-way consonants (ㄱ/ㅋ/ㄲ, ㄷ/ㅌ/ㄸ, ㅂ/ㅍ/ㅃ, ㅈ/ㅊ/ㅉ, ㅅ/ㅆ).** Indonesian contrasts voiced/voiceless
   (g/k); Korean contrasts plain / aspirated (puff) / tense (tight, no puff). A tissue held before the lips moves
   for ㅋ, not for ㄲ. Items: 가/카/까 (kids), 달/탈/딸 (adults).
4. **ㄹ rolled like Indonesian r.** Korean ㄹ is a light tap between vowels and an l at the end, never a trill.
5. **받침 released or followed by a vowel.** 밥 ends with closed lips, no "bapə"; final ㄱ is an unreleased k, not a
   glottal stop. Awareness: a 받침 moves into a following vowel (할아버지 ha-ra-beo-ji, 맛있어요 ma-si-sseo-yo) and
   several 받침 letters share one sound (옷 ot, 꽃 kkot).
6. **이에요/예요 mixed up.** 이에요 after a 받침 (공이에요), 예요 after a vowel (모자예요).
7. **Numbers from the wrong family.** Counting three apples with 삼 instead of 셋, or reading a price with 셋.
   (Before a counter, 하나 둘 셋 넷 shorten to 한 두 세 네: 한 개, 두 잔. Not taught at Pemula; 커피 하나 주세요 uses
   the full form, which is natural without a counter.)
8. **Politeness.** Using 안녕 or 미안해 with adults; 안녕히 가세요 vs 안녕히 계세요 swapped.
9. **Reading RR the Indonesian way.** "eo", "eu", "ae" are single vowels; "si" (시) sounds closer to "syi".

## 8. Open questions

1. **ㅇ under its trace shows "ng"** while the model answer says it is silent before a vowel. Alternative: show
   "ieung" (its name, which the speaker says). Kept "ng" so letter tiles in 공 read g + o + ng.

   "가 기역" style clip for kids.
3. **Aspirated consonants in unit 03, not 02** (unit theme table says "consonants" for 02). Done to keep unit 02 at
   9 traces and to teach ㅋ ㅌ ㅍ ㅊ next to the tense letters; easy to move back.
4. **No stroke keys for ㄲ ㄸ ㅃ ㅆ ㅉ or compound vowels.** Needed for Menengah.
5. **Pictures missing** for 화장실, 지하철, 택시 and a "bill"; adults use 차 with `pic_car` instead of 택시.
6. **이/가 dropped** in 화장실 어디예요? for beginners; the reviewer decides whether to teach 화장실이 어디예요? instead.
7. **Kids' 05 has no gem;** the framework asks for a gem surfacing in every unit — candidates for Menengah: Korean
   onomatopoeia, the consonant names from Choe Sejin's Hunmong Jahoe (1527).
