(77 items) and `.../ing-a/v1.json` (79 items), content versions `ing-k-pemula-0.1` / `ing-a-pemula-0.1`, every item
`review_status: draft`. Voice script: `android/content/voice/en.json` (179 clips, 1,716 characters). Contract:
`docs/content/INTERACTIONS_AUDIO.md` (picture-first rule, gestures, clips, unit themes §6); pack format:
`docs/content/AUTHORING.md`; outline: `docs/syllabus/01_BAHASA.md` §B. The packs are generated by a script

## 0. How this level works

- **Target `en`, instruction `id` only** (`instruction_langs: ["id"]`): the track is hidden from EN-profile learners,
  but every `{id, en}` text is still filled naturally so an English instruction mode can be switched on later.
- **American English** throughout, spelling and words (color, mom, transportation); the one exception the learner is
  told about is "the bill" (common in Indonesia and the UK) next to American "the check".
- **Less text** (contract §1): prompts come from one shared set of prompts of at most 6 words (kids use 15,
  adults 20; e.g. "Dengar, lalu pilih gambarnya."); the English word, the picture and the sound carry the question; model answers are one short line;
  each unit has one lesson screen with at most 3 short lines and 3–4 examples.
- **Picture / gesture / listening share** of PRACT items: kids 53/57 = **93 %**, adults 44/58 = **76 %** (pictures or
  sound alone: 98 % / 84 %). The rest are a few Yes/No checks, polite-reply choices, dialogue readings and "try it"
  cards.
- **Gestures:** `match_pairs` with `connect` (draw a line) or `drag` (picture onto word); `sort_buckets`, `order`,
  `tiles` and `fill_blank` with `drag`. Every drag/connect also works tap-then-tap (CR-A11Y).
- **Sound:** every English word or phrase on a card has a speaker clip (`audio`), and so does every English question
  line (`target_text`). Spelling tiles (single letters), sentence-building tiles and gap words carry no clip: the model
  answer shows the whole sentence. Wrong-English distractors ("Yes, I like.") are shown but **never voiced**, and
  in an item that has one, no answer card gets a speaker (QA pass 2026-10-18: the only card without a speaker would
  stand out); the question line keeps its clip. `audio_match` answer cards never carry a speaker.
  `audio_match` items (kids 14, adults 12) stay hidden until the ElevenLabs clips exist; each unit still has at least
  8 PRACT items without them, and no REMED item is an `audio_match`.
- **Picture cards** carry a bilingual `text` that is only their spoken (TalkBack) label; the picture is what shows.
- **Alignment with 01_BAHASA §B.** The contract fixes shared unit themes for all four languages, so the outline's
  units are regrouped, not dropped:

## 1. Kids (`ing-k`, items `ING-K-P-<unit>-<nnn>`)

Words are listed as *English — Indonesian — picture key*. Clip keys are `en/<word>` (e.g. `en/grandpa`,
`en/good_morning`, `en/the_cat_is_in_the_box`).

### 01 Halo, ini aku! / Hello & me — sticker `sticker_kite`

- **Words:** Hello! / Hi! — halo / hai — `pic_wave_hello`; Goodbye! / Bye! — sampai jumpa / dah — `pic_wave_bye`;
  Good morning! — selamat pagi — `pic_sun`; Good night! — selamat tidur — `sleep_moon`; Thank you! — terima kasih —
  `pic_thanks`; Sorry! — maaf — `pic_sorry`; Yes / No — ya / tidak (answer cards); eye, ear, nose, hand — mata,
  telinga, hidung, tangan — `sense_eye`, `sense_ear`, `sense_nose`, `sense_hand`; foot — kaki — `pic_foot`; head —
  kepala — `pic_head`; mouth — mulut — `body_child` (tap); boy — anak laki-laki — `pic_boy`; happy, sad, tired, hungry —
  senang, sedih, capek, lapar — `pic_happy`, `pic_sad`, `pic_tired`, `pic_hungry`.
- **Patterns:** *Hello! I am Sari.* · *I am a boy.* · *I'm happy. / I'm tired.*
- **Items:** pick the greeting picture; connect greetings to pictures; listen "Good morning!"; tap the nose on
  `body_child`; memory flip of body parts; sort greetings into *arriving* / *leaving* (picture baskets); Yes/No "happy?"
  with a sad face; drag feelings to words; build "I am a boy"; drag *am* into "Hello! I ___ Sari."
- **Watch for:** "I Sari" (no *am*: Indonesian has no linking verb); "Good night" used as a greeting.
- **Gem (`ING-K-P-01-013`):** "goodbye" was once "God be with ye". Source: Online Etymology Dictionary "goodbye";
  Merriam-Webster "goodbye".

### 02 Angka & warna / Numbers & colors — sticker `sticker_umbrella`

- **Words:** one … ten, twelve, fifteen (+ eleven in the gem) — `count_mango_*`, `count_egg_*`, `count_marble_*`;
  red, blue, yellow, green, black, white, orange, purple, brown, pink — merah, biru, kuning, hijau, hitam, putih,
  oranye, ungu, cokelat, merah muda — `pic_color_*`; plural cards cat/dogs, book/pens, ball/eggs, marble/marbles.
- **Patterns:** *I see three eggs.* · *one egg, two eggs*
- **Items:** pick 3 mangoes; connect marbles to number words; listen "seven", "fifteen"; order one–five; spell r-e-d
  with letter tiles; listen "purple"; memory flip of colors; pick 12 mangoes; drag *eggs* into "I see three ___.";
  sort word cards into *one* / *more than one* baskets (1 marble vs 5 marbles); REMED drag-match of blue, black,
  orange, brown.
- **Watch for:** missing plural -s ("three egg"): Indonesian marks plurals with numbers or reduplication, not -s.
- **Gem (`ING-K-P-02-012`):** eleven and twelve once meant "one left" and "two left" (after counting ten). Source:
  Online Etymology Dictionary "eleven", "twelve".

### 03 Keluarga & teman / Family & friends — sticker `sticker_angklung`

- **Words:** mom — ibu — `fam_mother`; dad — ayah — `fam_father`; sister / brother — saudara perempuan / laki-laki —
  `fam_sister` / `fam_brother`; grandma / grandpa — nenek / kakek — `fam_grandmother` / `fam_grandfather`; baby —
  bayi — `fam_baby`; boy, girl, man, woman — `pic_boy`, `pic_girl`, `pic_man`, `pic_woman`; friends — teman-teman —
  `pic_friends`; teacher — guru — `pic_teacher`; doctor — dokter — `pic_doctor`; he / she.
- **Patterns:** *This is my mom.* · *He is my dad.* · *She is my sister.* · *She is my grandma.*
- **Items:** pick mom; connect family pictures; listen "grandpa", "friends", "This is my mom."; sort family pictures
  into *he* / *she* baskets; build "He is my dad"; Yes/No "brother?" with a sister; drag *She* into "___ is my grandma."
- **Watch for:** he/she mixed up (Indonesian *dia* is both); brother/sister do not say older or younger (kakak/adik).

### 04 Makanan & minuman / Food & drink — sticker `sticker_mango`

- **Words:** rice — nasi — `pic_rice_bowl`; noodles — mi — `pic_noodle_bowl`; bread — roti — `pic_bread`; egg — telur —
  `pic_egg_fried`; cake — kue — `pic_cake`; chicken, fish — ayam, ikan — `pic_chicken_dish`, `pic_fish_dish`; vegetables —
  sayur — `pic_vegetables`; soup — sup — `pic_soup`; apple, banana, mango, watermelon — `pic_apple`, `fruit_banana`,
  `fruit_mango`, `pic_watermelon`; water, milk, tea, juice — `drink_water`, `pic_milk_glass`, `pic_tea_cup`,
  `pic_juice`; food / drink.
- **Patterns:** *I like apples.* · *I don't like fish.* · *I'm hungry!*
- **Items:** pick rice; connect fruit; listen "noodles", "water", "I like juice."; spell c-a-k-e; sort pictures into
  *food* / *drink*; memory flip of dishes; drag *don't* into "I ___ like fish."; build "I like apples".
- **Watch for:** "I not like" / "I no like" (Indonesian *tidak* goes straight before the verb); "I like apple" for
  apples in general (the model answer shows *apples*; not tested at this level).

### 05 Hewan & benda / Animals & things — sticker `sticker_cat`

- **Words:** cat, dog, bird, fish, cow, chicken, rabbit, horse, goat, duck, elephant, monkey — `pic_cat` … `pic_monkey`;
  book, pencil, bag, chair, door, ball, shoes, hat — `pic_book` … `pic_hat`; kite — layang-layang —
  `scene_kampung_shapes` (tap); in, on, under — `pos_cat_inside`, `pos_cat_above` (cat sitting on the box),
  `pos_cat_below` (cat under the table).
- **Patterns:** *The cat is in the box.* · *The cat is on the box.* · *The cat is under the table.*
- **Items:** pick dog; connect farm animals; listen "elephant", "ball", "The cat is on the box."; spell c-a-t; memory
  flip of animals; sort pictures into *animals* / *things*; tap the kite in the kampung scene; pick "in the box";
  drag *under* into "The cat is ___ the table."; try at home: label door, chair and bed.
- **Watch for:** "in" vs "on" (Indonesian *di* covers both).
- **Gem (`ING-K-P-05-013`):** "orangutan" comes from Malay *orang* + *hutan*. Source: Online Etymology Dictionary
  "orangutan"; Merriam-Webster "orangutan".

**Daily puzzles:** `ING-K-P-DP-01` riddle "my English name starts with C and I say meow" (cat / cow / chicken);
`ING-K-P-DP-02` red + yellow paint = orange.

## 2. Adults (`ing-a`, items `ING-A-P-<unit>-<nnn>`)

### 01 Menyapa & berkenalan / Hello & me — sticker `sticker_book`

- **Phrases:** Hello! · Goodbye! · Good morning! (`pic_sun`) · Good evening! · Good night! (`sleep_moon`) · See you
  later! · Have a nice day! · Nice to meet you. / Nice to meet you, too. (`pic_friends`) · Hi! How are you? · I'm fine,
  thanks. And you? · I'm good, thank you. · Thank you! / You're welcome. (`pic_thanks`) · I'm sorry. (`pic_sorry`) ·
  Yes, please. · My name is … · I'm from Medan. · I'm a doctor / a teacher (`pic_doctor`, `pic_teacher`, `pic_cook`).
- **Patterns:** *My name is Dewi.* · *I'm from + city.* · *I'm a + job.* · *Can I have some water, please?*
- **Dialogue (`R-INGA-01`, `pic_friends`):** Rina: Hi! I'm Rina. I'm from Medan. / Budi: Nice to meet you, Rina.
  I'm Budi. → "Where is Rina from?"
- **Watch for:** "Good night" as a greeting (it closes the evening; *Good evening* opens it); "I'm doctor" (no *a*);
  "My name Dewi" (no *is*); replying "Thank you too" (*terima kasih kembali*) instead of *You're welcome*.
- **Gem (`ING-A-P-01-013`):** "OK" began in 1839 Boston as "oll korrect", a joke spelling of "all correct". Source:
  Merriam-Webster "OK"; Online Etymology Dictionary "OK" (research of Allen Walker Read).

### 02 Angka & warna / Numbers & colors — sticker `sticker_drum`

- **Words:** eight, eleven, fifteen, fifty, five hundred, five thousand; thirteen/thirty, fourteen/forty,
  sixteen/sixty (digits as text cards); ten colors `pic_color_*`; money picture `note_generic`.
- **Patterns:** *How much is it?* · *It's fifteen thousand rupiah.* · *twenty thousand rupiah* (no -s).
- **Dialogue (`R-INGA-02`, `pic_coffee_cup`):** Buyer: How much is the coffee? / Seller: It's fifteen thousand
  rupiah. → Rp15.000, Rp50.000 or Rp5.000?
- **Watch for:** thirteen vs thirty (stress: thir-TEEN vs THIR-ty); "twenty thousands"; "How many is it?" for a
  price; writing Rp15.000 in English as Rp15,000.
- **Gem (`ING-A-P-02-013`):** "million" came into English through French from the old Italian *millione*, "a great
  thousand" (QA pass 2026-10-18: the French step added, as etymonline gives it). Source: Online Etymology Dictionary
  "million"; Merriam-Webster "million".

### 03 Keluarga & teman / Family & friends — sticker `sticker_lantern`

- **Words:** mother, father, brother, sister, grandmother, grandfather, baby — `fam_*`; husband, wife, son, daughter
  (sorted onto `pic_man` / `pic_woman` baskets); his, her.
- **Patterns:** *I have a brother.* · *She has two children.* · *My father has a motorbike.* · *His name is Andi. /
  Her name is Sari.* · *This is my brother. He is a doctor.*
- **Dialogue (`R-INGA-03`, `pic_friends`, at an arisan):** Ani: Do you have children? / Tono: Yes, I have a son and a
  daughter. → "Tono has two children." Yes/No.
- **Watch for:** "She have" (third-person -s); he/she and his/her mixed up (*dia*, *-nya*); *daughter* said with a
  "g" (the gh is silent).

### 04 Makanan & minuman / Food & drink — sticker `sticker_teapot`

- **Words:** chicken, fish, egg, rice, noodles, soup, vegetables, bread, coffee, tea, juice, milk — `pic_*`; food /
  drinks; fried rice (nasi goreng) and iced tea (es teh) on the screen.
- **Patterns:** *Can I have …, please?* · *Iced tea, please.* · *Can I have the bill, please?* · *I like tea.* ·
  *I don't like coffee.* · *Do you like spicy food? — Yes, I do.*
- **Dialogue (`R-INGA-04`, `pic_noodle_bowl`):** Customer: Can I have some noodles and a tea, please? / Seller: Sure.
  That's thirty thousand rupiah. → which drink (picture); "It's thirteen thousand rupiah." Yes/No (teen/ty again).
- **Watch for:** "I am not like coffee"; "Yes, I like." as a short answer; "Give me …" as an order (shown as the tile
  distractor *give*; *Can I have …* is the polite form).
- **Gem (`ING-A-P-04-012`):** "ketchup" most likely comes from Malay *kĕchap* (a fish sauce), possibly from Hokkien.
  Source: Merriam-Webster "ketchup"; Online Etymology Dictionary "ketchup".

### 05 Tempat & perjalanan / Places & getting around — sticker `sticker_bicycle`

- **Words:** market, station, park, school, hospital — `pic_market`, `pic_station`, `pic_park`, `pic_school`,
  `pic_hospital`; bus, train, car, motorbike, bicycle, plane, ship — `pic_*`; ojek (= motorbike taxi); places /
  transportation; turn right / turn left / go straight; next to, near.
- **Patterns:** *I go to work by bus.* · *I take an ojek.* · *Excuse me, where is the station?* · *It's next to the
  market.* · *It's near the park.* · *Let's meet at the park.*
- **Dialogue (`R-INGA-05`, `pic_station`):** Rudi: Excuse me, where is the station? / Woman: Go straight. It's next
  to the market. → what is next to the station (picture).
- **Watch for:** "with bus" (*naik bus* = *by bus*); answering *where* with *how* ("By bus.") or with time.

**Daily puzzles:** `ING-A-P-DP-01` which word reads the same backwards (noon / moon / soon); `ING-A-P-DP-02` change
from fifty thousand for a thirty-five-thousand coffee (fifteen thousand rupiah).

## 3. Common mistakes of Indonesian speakers at this level

| Mistake | Why it happens | Practised in |
|---|---|---|
| "I Sari." / "My name Dewi." | Indonesian has no linking verb (*Saya Sari*) | K-01-011, A-01-006 |
| "I am agree", "I am not like coffee" | *be* added to every verb, the mirror image of the above | A-04-010 (the *am agree* form itself comes at Menengah) |
| "Yes, I like." / "Yes, I am." | Short answers echo the auxiliary: *Do you…? — Yes, I do.* | A-04-015, A-04-008 |
| "I not like fish" | *tidak* goes straight before the verb | K-04-009, K-04-014 |
| "three egg", "twenty thousands" | Plural shown by number or reduplication; *thousand* never takes -s after a number | K-02-009/010/015, A-02-011 |
| "She have two children" | No verb agreement in Indonesian | A-03-005, A-03-006, A-03-013 |
| he/she, his/her mixed up | *dia* and *-nya* do not mark gender | K-03-004/006/008/010, A-03-007/008/011 |
| "I'm doctor" | No articles in Indonesian | A-01-007, A-01-008 |
| "How many is it?" | *berapa* covers both much and many | A-02-009 |
| "I go with bus" | *naik bus* translated word by word | A-05-004, A-05-014 |
| "Good night" on arrival | *selamat malam* is used both ways | A-01-002, A-01-003, K-01-008 |
| 13 vs 30 heard or said wrong | Stress and the -teen/-ty ending carry the difference | A-02-002/003/006, A-02-015, A-04-007 |
| *th* said as t/d (three, thank, this, mother) | No /θ/ or /ð/ in Indonesian | heard in the clips (no speaking task in the app); voice notes ask for a clear *th* |
| final consonants dropped (*eggs*, *has*, *likes*) | Indonesian syllables rarely end in clusters | clips of *eggs*, *Tono has two children* |
| False friends in scope: *hand* ≠ arm, *foot* ≠ leg (*tangan*, *kaki* cover both); *brother/sister* ≠ older/younger; *rice* = nasi, beras and padi | One Indonesian word, two English ones (or the reverse) | K-01 body items; noted for the next body unit |

## 4. Reviewer checklist

1. **English (native reviewer):** every word, phrase and dialogue is natural, current American English at A1;
   short answers and contractions are right; nothing reads as translated. Check the deliberate choices: "the bill"
   (with a note on "the check"), "a tea", "some noodles", "Good evening!" with an exclamation mark, "transportation".
2. **Clips:** listen to all 179 clips in `voice/en.json` once generated (one voice, slow and clear, American). Pay
   attention to the -teen/-ty pairs (stress), *daughter*, *ojek* (said the Indonesian way), and that the texts with
   "!" sound friendly, not shouted. No wrong-English sentence is voiced (`bad_*` cards have no clip).
3. **Indonesian (language reviewer):** EYD V; "kamu" for kids, "Anda" for adults; glosses in model answers are
   correct (*saudara laki-laki/perempuan* for brother/sister; *capek* is informal on purpose for kids).
4. **Pictures (when the `pic_*` set is drawn):** `pic_sun` reads as morning, `sleep_moon` as night/bedtime;
   `pic_teacher`, `pic_doctor` and `pic_cook` work as jobs and are not tied to a gender (no item uses he/she with
   them); `fam_sister` reads as a girl and `fam_brother` as a boy; `pos_cat_above` = cat *on* the box,
   `pos_cat_below` = cat *under the table*; `note_generic` works as "money / the bill"; the 1-marble and 5-marble
   pictures work as *one* / *more than one* baskets.
5. **Answers:** each choice has exactly one right answer that a careful learner can reach from the picture, the
   sound or the dialogue; tiles never contain two cards with the same text (scoring compares card ids).
6. **Gems:** check the three facts per pack against the sources in each item's `source` (goodbye, eleven/twelve,
   orangutan; OK, million, ketchup). The kids "goodbye" gem mentions God: confirm the wording is fine for every
   religion. Cut a gem that cannot be verified.
7. **Contract:** run `fu11/en/check.py` (prompt length, share, art and clip keys, REMED per skill, gestures) and
   `validate.sh`; until the art agent lands the `pic_*` drawings, only "unknown art key pic_…" may remain.
8. **Before release:** set `review_status` to `approved` item by item; bump `content_version` if either language or
   a clip text changes (and regenerate the voice script).
